Oil & gas

The Commercial Movement of Energy.

The energy business is ultimately about movement: of crude oil and refined products, of commitments between counterparties, and of the capital that makes both possible.

SWEP’s oil and gas interests span that movement, with a focus on opportunities that are commercially sound, properly structured and supported by credible partners. Four areas define the company’s activity.

01

Crude Oil

International sourcing, supply and trading opportunities.

SWEP’s interest in crude oil centers on the commercial fundamentals of supply: the origin and specification of the crude, the logistics that connect it with refining demand, and the standing of every counterparty involved. Opportunities are assessed against those fundamentals before any commitment is made.

  • Origin and specification
  • Logistics and delivery
  • Counterparty standing
  • Contractual structure
02

Petroleum Products

Selected refined-product trading and supply opportunities.

Refined products move through a more demanding chain than crude, with specification, storage and delivery windows shaping commercial outcomes. SWEP focuses on products and routes where supply, demand and logistics align, and where commercial terms reflect the realities of the market.

  • Product specification
  • Storage and delivery windows
  • Route economics
  • Qualified buyers and suppliers
03

Energy Infrastructure

Strategic infrastructure supporting the movement, storage and distribution of energy.

Terminals, storage, pipelines and the logistics that connect them determine whether energy reaches its market reliably. SWEP’s interest in infrastructure reflects a simple view: durable value in energy is built on the assets that move it.

  • Terminals and storage
  • Pipelines and gathering
  • Marine logistics
  • Distribution
04

Energy Partnerships

Long-term relationships with qualified energy companies and counterparties.

Significant energy transactions are rarely completed alone. SWEP works toward partnerships built on clear terms, appropriate diligence and a shared view of value: relationships designed to outlast any single transaction.

  • Qualified counterparties
  • Clear commercial terms
  • Appropriate due diligence
  • Long-term alignment
